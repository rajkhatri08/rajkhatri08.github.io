A defect nobody had cracked

Raw data to business decision, end to end — messy inputs, an explicit method, a falsifiable hypothesis, physical validation, a shipped artifact, and a stakeholder review.

TATA MOTORS  /  DATA ANALYTICS INTERN  /  JUL–AUG 2026  /  PANTNAGAR, UTTARAKHAND  /  BODY-IN-WHITE QUALITY

01 — PROBLEM

A Body-in-White dimensional defect in the weld shop had gone unresolved for a long time — long enough that a clean, data-driven answer wasn't really expected anymore. No labelled dataset, no clean schema; inspection records collected for compliance, not analysis.

02 — METHOD

Applied statistical trend analysis — linear regression with R² confidence scoring — across roughly 300 inspection data points. The working hypothesis was falsifiable by design: if on-line equipment wear were the cause, the pattern would drift with time on the line. It didn't. That ruled wear out and pointed upstream.

03 — VALIDATION

Took the statistical finding onto the shop floor and tested it against physical reality — direct measurement of the parts. The measurement matched the prediction, isolating a supplier-side dimensional issue. A model that agrees with itself proves nothing; this one had to agree with a gauge.

04 — DELIVERY

Designed a containment procedure requiring no production stoppage or vendor dependency, built an interactive dashboard tracking 10 quality metrics, authored a 20+ section technical report benchmarked against DFMEA and Six Sigma frameworks, and presented the findings directly to the BIW Shop Head and quality stakeholders — where the method, not just the result, had to hold up to questioning.
